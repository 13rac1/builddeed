Shareware is back, but you can resell your license.

BuildDeed sells software licenses as NFTs on NEAR. A license gives you the verified official build of a program whose source code is public. When you no longer need it, you sell it, and the developers and contributors get a share of that sale too.

The problem

Open source developers give away their work, and most of them are not paid for it. Closed licenses pay, but they hide the code, and you cannot sell a license that you no longer use.

BuildDeed keeps the code public. Anyone can read it and build it. The license buys the official build: the binary that the developers built, attested and recorded on chain, with updates for a year. That is what shareware sold, and it worked.

How it works

  1. Download the official app. It runs, and it shows the shareware screen. The folder check is off.
  2. Buy a license. One wallet approval. The license contract mints an NFT to your account and pays the developers, the contributors and a 3% BuildDeed fee.
  3. Register your computer. The app opens this site with its computer id. One wallet approval gives the computer a seat on your license; a license has three. The app finds its seat on chain and the screen goes away.
  4. Resell it. List your license on the market. The buyer pays, a 10% royalty goes to the developers and contributors, and you get the rest. The sale clears your seats, so your computers go back to unregistered.
  5. Verify any download. Each official file has a SHA-256 on chain, with a link to its build attestation. Drop a file on the verify page to check it.

What NEAR does here

The license is an NFT that pays people

Every sale, first or resale, splits the payment in the contract among the developer and the contributors. Nobody holds the money in between.

The contract is the record of official builds

The developer records the commit and the hash of each release file. A recorded file cannot be changed or removed, so a download can be checked years later.

The license holds its computer seats

The NFT records which computers use it: three seats, moved by the owner, cleared by a resale. The app asks a public NEAR RPC node for its seat. No company has to keep a license server running for your software to work.

For developers

You keep the code public and choose the terms: price, update period, royalty, and who gets paid. Your release pipeline builds in a container, attests each file and records its hash with a key that can call only one contract method. Anyone can rebuild the Linux and Windows files and get the same hash.
