The problem

Open source developers give away their work, and most of them are not paid for it. Closed licenses pay, but they lock the code, and you cannot sell a license that you no longer use.

BuildDeed keeps the code open. Anyone can read it and build it. The license buys the official build: the binary that the developers built, attested and recorded on chain, with updates for a year. That is what shareware sold, and it worked.

How it works

  1. Download the official app. It runs, and it shows the shareware screen. The folder check is off.
  2. Buy a license. One wallet approval. The license contract mints an NFT to your account and pays the developers, the contributors and a 3% BuildDeed fee.
  3. Register the app. The app opens this site, your wallet signs a message (no transaction, no fee), and the app checks on chain that your account owns a license. The screen goes away.
  4. Resell it. List your license on the market. The buyer pays, a 10% royalty goes to the developers and contributors, and you get the rest. Your copy of the app goes back to unregistered.
  5. Verify any download. Each official file has a SHA-256 on chain, with a link to its build attestation. Drop a file on the verify page to check it.

What NEAR does here

The license is an NFT that pays people

Every sale, first or resale, splits the payment in the contract among the developer and the contributors. Nobody holds the money in between.

The contract is the record of official builds

The developer records the commit and the hash of each release file. A recorded file cannot be changed or removed, so a download can be checked years later.

The app checks the chain, not a license server

The app asks a public NEAR RPC node whether your account owns a license for its version. No company has to keep a server running for your software to work.

For developers

You keep the code open and choose the terms: price, update period, royalty, and who gets paid. Your release pipeline builds in a container, attests each file and records its hash with a key that can call only one contract method. Anyone can rebuild the Linux and Windows files and get the same hash.
